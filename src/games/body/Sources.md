# Body Explorer references

Checked 13 October 2026. Child-facing text is paraphrased and translated into Dutch. Organ functions are deliberately distinctive: filtering blood to make urine is separate from storing urine; absorbing nutrients is separate from absorbing water from leftovers. These are selected elementary functions, not exhaustive accounts of every organ’s roles.

- [NIH/NIDDK: Your Digestive System & How It Works](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works): stomach mixing, liver bile, pancreatic digestive enzymes, small-intestine nutrient absorption, large-intestine water absorption and stool formation.
- [NIH/NIDDK: Diabetes, Endocrinology and Metabolic Diseases (2020 research report)](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/-/media/Files/Strategic-Plans/2020_NIDDK_RecentAdvancesEmergingOpps_DEM.pdf): the pancreas produces both insulin and digestive enzymes.
- [NIH/NIDDK: The Urinary Tract & How It Works](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/urinary-tract-how-it-works): paired kidneys filter blood and make urine, bladder stores urine; urinary-system illustration.
- [NIH/NHLBI: How the Heart Works](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart): blood circulation and heart position.
- [NIH/NHLBI: How the Lungs Work](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/lungs): oxygen enters blood and carbon dioxide leaves it.
- [NIH/NHLBI: The Respiratory System](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/lungs/respiratory-system): paired lungs, left lung smaller to accommodate the heart.
- [NIH/NCI: Brain](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-terms/def/brain): control of body functions.
- [NIH curriculum: Information about the Brain](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK20367/): conscious thought, sensory and motor functions.
- [NIH/NCI SEER: Introduction to the Skeletal System](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/skeletal/): support, protection, movement.
- [NIH/NCI SEER: Divisions of the Skeleton](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/skeletal/divisions/): axial and appendicular skeleton, labeled skeletal illustrations.

Diagrams are original SVG schematics, not diagnostic images. They show a simplified front view with rear abdominal organs made visible. The liver is on the person’s right (picture left), stomach on the person’s left (picture right), heart between the lungs slightly to the person’s left. Both kidneys are shown laterally, the right slightly lower, and the pancreas behind/below the stomach. Highlighting lifts a target above neighboring shapes so children can inspect it. Bone questions use parts/groups of the skeleton, with paired limb bones shown together.
